Zazen

座 禅

Seated Meditation

Zazen, literally seated meditation, is a meditative discipline that is typically the primary practice of the Zen Buddhist tradition.

The meaning and method of zazen vary from school to school. Still, in general, it can be regarded as a means of insight into the nature of existence. In the Japanese Rinzai school, zazen is usually associated with the study of koans. The Soto School of Japan, on the other hand, only rarely incorporates koans into zazen, preferring an approach where the mind has no object at all, known as shikantaza.

Zazen is considered the heart of Japanese Soto Zen Buddhist practice. The aim of zazen is just sitting, that is, suspending all judgmental thinking and letting words, ideas, images, and thoughts pass by without getting involved in them. Shikantaza is a form of meditation, in which the practitioner does not use any specific object of meditation; rather, practitioners remain as much as possible in the present moment, aware of and observing what passes through their minds and around them.

"Sitting fixedly, think of not thinking. How do you think of not thinking? Nonthinking. This is the art of zazen."

Dogen, Shobogenzo

Meditation Instructions

Zazen (Seated Meditation)

Basic Technique:

  1. Sit on a cushion (zafu) with your legs crossed
  2. Keep your back straight and head upright
  3. Place your hands in the cosmic mudra (left hand on right, thumbs touching)
  4. Keep your eyes half-open, gazing downward
  5. Breathe naturally through your nose
  6. Keep your mouth closed, tongue against the roof of your mouth
Tips for Beginners
  • Start with shorter sessions (10-15 minutes)
  • Use a comfortable cushion to maintain proper posture
  • Don't worry about clearing your mind - just observe your thoughts
  • If you feel pain, adjust your position gently

Kinhin (Walking Meditation)

Basic Technique:

  1. Stand up slowly from your zazen position
  2. Make a fist with your left hand
  3. Cover the fist with your right hand
  4. Hold your hands at chest level
  5. Walk slowly, taking half steps
  6. Coordinate your breathing with your steps
  7. Keep your eyes slightly open, gazing ahead
Tips for Kinhin
  • Walk in a straight line, back and forth
  • Maintain the same mindful awareness as in zazen
  • Keep your movements slow and deliberate
  • Use this time to stretch your legs between sitting sessions
